How salons and barbershops can reduce missed appointment enquiries

A practical guide for Kenyan salons, barbershops and service businesses to organise service menus, staff availability, appointment requests and follow-up.

By TWIGA · 7 November 2026 · Practical business guide

In a busy salon, the person who answers the phone may also be washing hair, braiding, serving a walk-in or taking payment. A missed call can mean a missed appointment—but rushing to answer every call can interrupt the service already in progress.

Start with a clear service menu

List each service using the names customers actually use. Include the price or price range, expected duration, whether a consultation is needed and any preparation the customer should know about. If prices vary by hair length, style or product use, say so instead of giving the AI permission to guess.

Make staff availability visible

Appointment availability should come from a calendar or scheduling system that the team keeps up to date. Define working hours, breaks, leave, chair or room capacity, service duration and buffers between appointments. If two staff members can perform a service, record that explicitly.

Use a booking confirmation checklist

  • Customer name and a reliable callback number.
  • Requested service and any relevant preferences.
  • Preferred date and time.
  • Assigned stylist or barber, if applicable.
  • Confirmation message and cancellation or rescheduling instructions.
Never confirm a slot from memory. The booking system should check availability at the moment the appointment is saved, and check again if another booking may have taken the slot.

Keep a human handover for exceptions

New styles, bridal work, allergies, complaints, refunds, deposits and special requests may need a person. Decide which questions the receptionist can answer and which must be passed to the owner or manager.

Follow up without spamming

Appointment reminders can reduce no-shows when they are useful, timely and sent through a channel the customer agreed to use. Keep messages short, identify the business, include the appointment details and offer a simple way to change the booking. Respect opt-outs and do not send repeated promotional messages just because a number is in the customer list.

Where TWIGA fits

TWIGA is designed to help a salon organise customer enquiries, booking requests and follow-up workflows. A live booking flow depends on connecting the business calendar and configuring services, staff availability and permissions. The AI must not invent free slots or silently change a confirmed appointment.

Before going live, test at least: a normal booking, a slot that has just been taken, a reschedule, a cancellation, an after-hours enquiry, a Kiswahili/English mixed request, an unclear service name and a request to speak to staff. Keep the team involved during the first weeks so the system can be corrected safely.

A simple first-week plan

  1. Document the top ten questions customers ask.
  2. Enter the current services, prices and durations.
  3. Connect one calendar and verify the staff schedule.
  4. Run staff-only tests before letting customers use the flow.
  5. Review every booking and handover daily, then improve the rules.
