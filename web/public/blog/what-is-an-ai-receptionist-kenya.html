What is an AI receptionist, and how can it help a Kenyan business?

An AI receptionist is software that can respond to configured customer enquiries, collect useful details and help with bookings. Here is what Kenyan business owners should know before adopting one.

By TWIGA · 7 November 2026 · Practical business guide

A customer calls while you are serving someone at the counter. Another asks whether you are open. Someone else wants to know the price of a service or whether Friday afternoon is available. For a small team, answering every enquiry immediately can be difficult.

An AI receptionist is a software assistant designed to handle parts of that first customer conversation. Depending on its integrations and rules, it can answer common questions, collect a caller's details, help arrange an appointment and pass more complicated requests to a person.

The important distinction: an AI receptionist is not automatically connected to every phone line or business system. Call routing, voice services, calendars and messaging channels must be configured and tested before those actions are live.

What can an AI receptionist actually do?

  • Answer common questions: explain opening hours, location, service details and published prices that the business has approved.
  • Capture enquiries: collect a name, contact number, reason for calling and the best time to respond.
  • Support appointment requests: check connected calendar availability and follow the business's booking rules.
  • Send follow-ups: where a supported messaging or notification integration has been connected and the customer has consented.
  • Hand over to staff: route complaints, sensitive requests, uncertain answers or callers who ask for a person.

Why this matters for Kenyan businesses

Many Kenyan businesses are owner-operated or have small teams. A salonist may be working with a client, a mechanic may be under a vehicle, a clinic receptionist may be checking in a patient, and a shopkeeper may be serving a queue. The phone does not always ring at a convenient moment.

A well-configured receptionist can help make enquiries more consistent. It can also give the owner a record of what needs attention instead of relying on memory or scattered WhatsApp messages.

Does it understand English and Kiswahili?

Language support depends on the speech recognition, voice and assistant configuration selected. English and Kiswahili can be part of a Kenya-focused setup, but local accents, code-switching, names, locations and industry vocabulary need real testing with consenting speakers. Do not assume that a language label alone guarantees accurate understanding.

What should you check before switching it on?

  1. Confirm which phone number and call-routing method will deliver calls to the assistant.
  2. Write down approved answers, service prices, opening hours and escalation rules.
  3. Connect only the calendars, contacts and business systems the assistant needs.
  4. Test common questions, noisy environments, interruptions and misunderstood names.
  5. Make sure callers can reach a person and know when they are speaking with an automated assistant.
  6. Set a clear policy for recordings, retention, access and customer consent.

How TWIGA approaches the job

TWIGA is being built as a business receptionist and operations companion for Kenyan businesses. The product direction brings together customer enquiries, appointment workflows, business contacts, orders and a dashboard for reviewing activity. Some workflows require an external voice provider, a supported call route or a connected Google account before they can operate.

That requires a supported carrier, call-routing or telephony integration. We prefer to explain that limitation clearly rather than promise a feature that a phone operating system does not allow a normal app to perform.

Start with one useful workflow

Choose the most common missed enquiry in your business. Configure the approved answer, decide when staff should take over, and test it with real examples before expanding. Automation works best when it makes a reliable process easier—not when it hides a broken one.
